COURSE-BASED PROJECT · DATA MINING

Data Preprocessing and Outlier Detection for Improving Classification Accuracy

Clean the data. Challenge the assumptions. Measure the outcome.

01 / What this project actually does

A local experiment workbench compares the same classifiers under three conditions: minimal imputation and encoding; full preprocessing; and full preprocessing with training-only outlier removal. It saves the exact split, settings, cross-validation scores, held-out predictions, filtering decisions, transformed training records, and selected pipeline.

The reference DMFINAL.pdf uses scikit-learn pipelines for movie classification. This project follows that architectural pattern, but changes the research question to preprocessing and outlier detection. No movie dataset, claimed movie results, or fabricated accuracy figures are reused.

04 / Experiment design

  1. Normalize empty categorical strings and non-finite numeric values. Remove missing labels and exact duplicate feature rows. Reject identical features carrying conflicting labels.
  2. Create a seeded, stratified train/test split. All methods share the exact same indices. This app assumes independent records; patient/grouped/time-series datasets require a group-aware or time-aware splitter instead.
  3. Create 3 or 5 stratified folds within training data. Numeric medians and categorical modes are fitted on each fold’s training subset. Unknown categories are ignored by one-hot encoding at inference.
  4. Fit each chosen numeric-only outlier detector on the fold’s training subset, without target labels. Remove only flagged training rows, then refit classification preprocessing on retained rows.
  5. Fit scaling and optional PCA inside the fold. Evaluate on all validation rows, including unusual ones.
  6. Choose the model/treatment with the highest mean training-CV macro-F1. Refit on the full training partition and then evaluate on every held-out test record.
  7. Save positive, negative, and zero differences. The dashboard’s accuracy delta compares the selected arm with the same classifier’s minimal baseline.
Why not “raw” data?

Most classifiers cannot accept missing values or strings. A fair runnable baseline therefore includes median/mode imputation and one-hot encoding, but no scaling, PCA, or outlier removal. Numeric outlier detection is standardized separately for distance-based methods; categorical features remain available to the classifier.

Tree depth is capped at 7 with three examples per leaf. KNN uses five distance-weighted neighbors. SVM uses an RBF kernel with C = 1 and probability estimation. Random forest uses 100 trees and two examples per leaf. The MLP uses hidden layers of 48 and 24 units, backpropagation, Adam, and up to 400 iterations; convergence warnings are surfaced explicitly.

07 / Data & safety boundaries

Use the built-in synthetic customer dataset for a mixed-type demonstration. It has fixed seeded missingness and injected feature anomalies; labels are never corrupted. The 12 deliberate duplicate rows exercise cleaning. Iris, Wine, and Wisconsin breast cancer are bundled scikit-learn educational benchmarks and require no download.

For a CSV, select a 2–15-class target and exclude IDs and target-derived features. Limits: 8 MB, 5,000 records, 60 input features, at least 50 distinct labeled records and eight records per class. Categorical features with more than 30 categories are rejected to avoid accidental ID/free-text encoding. At least one numeric feature is required for outlier comparisons.

A detector is excluded if it would leave fewer than 20 rows, remove over half the training rows, eliminate a class, or leave fewer than three examples in any class. These are operational safety checks, not proof that the remaining data are fair or representative. Minority-class removal should be reviewed using the training audit export.

Exported source_row values are zero-based positions in the original data records, excluding the CSV header, even after missing-label or duplicate removal. Split train_ids and test_ids refer to cleaned records; train_source_rows and test_source_rows map them back to the original input. New runs include this explicit mapping. Legacy runs created before this audit used cleaned positions in source_row; their saved files remain unchanged.

The insight panel separately compares the CV-ranked outlier arm with its matched minimal baseline and its matched preprocessed arm. Only the latter isolates the added effect of outlier removal.

All data stay in this local folder. Uploads and results are not automatically deleted. Avoid real personal or confidential data, especially on shared machines. The Flask development server binds to localhost and is for demonstrations, not production deployment. Joblib files must only be loaded from trusted local sources. The app does not accept model uploads.

08 / How to present it

  1. Inspect the messy demo and point out missing values, duplicates, and mixed feature types.
  2. Explain why preprocessing must fit after splitting, and inside each CV fold.
  3. Run the default five classifiers against all five detectors. Compare A/B/C treatment bars for the CV-selected classifier.
  4. Explore the outlier landscape and switch detectors. Explain why a candidate anomaly is not automatically an erroneous record.
  5. Read the confusion matrix, macro-F1, and balanced accuracy rather than citing accuracy alone.
  6. Download the comparison CSV and training audit. Use the same seed to reproduce the split and metrics.
  7. State the actual finding, including any lack of improvement. Do not claim causal effects, clinical validity, statistical significance, or general superiority from one split.
